
# Projected Changes in Invasive Plant Suitability Across Africa

## Project Overview

This project examines current and projected future patterns of climatic suitability overlap among four invasive plant species across Africa.

Using species distribution modeling outputs and spatial analysis in ArcGIS Pro, the project evaluates how climate change may alter the geographic distribution and overlap of suitable habitats by 2061–2080 under SSP370.

The analysis identifies areas where multiple invasive species may find suitable climatic conditions, highlighting potential regions of increasing invasion risk.

## Study Species

Four invasive plant species were examined:

- Chromolaena odorata
- Leucaena leucocephala
- Mimosa pigra
- Opuntia stricta

## Objectives

- Map current climatic suitability overlap among four invasive plant species.
- Project future suitability overlap under SSP370 (2061–2080).
- Identify geographic areas experiencing increases or decreases in the number of species with suitable habitat.
- Quantify changes in suitable habitat overlap across Africa.

## Data Sources

- Species occurrence records: Global Biodiversity Information Facility (GBIF).
- Current climate data: WorldClim version 2.1.
- Future climate data: CMIP6 projections under SSP370 (2061–2080).
- Coordinate reference system: Africa Albers Equal Area Conic.

## Methods

1. Used species distribution modeling outputs to represent climatically suitable areas for four invasive plant species.
2. Combined binary suitability maps to calculate the number of species with suitable habitat in each geographic area.
3. Generated current and future species overlap maps.
4. Calculated changes in species overlap between current and future climatic conditions.
5. Summarized the geographic area associated with different levels of species overlap.
6. Created a comparative cartographic layout using ArcGIS Pro.

## Key Findings

- Suitable habitat overlap is concentrated in parts of West, Central, and East Africa.
- Future projections indicate geographic expansion of suitable habitat for multiple invasive species.
- Some regions show increases in the number of species with suitable climatic conditions, while others show decreases.
- The results suggest that climate change may redistribute areas potentially vulnerable to multiple plant invasions.
