
# East Gallatin River: Parcels, Structures, and Riparian Setback

## Project Overview

This project examines the spatial relationships among land parcels, structures, selected subdivisions, and a 300-foot riparian setback along the East Gallatin River in Montana.

Using ArcGIS Pro, the project integrates geographic datasets and high-resolution aerial imagery to visualize land development near the river corridor and demonstrate the application of GIS in riparian conservation and land-use planning.

## Objectives

- Map the East Gallatin River and surrounding waterways.
- Visualize the 300-foot river setback.
- Display land parcels and structures near the river.
- Identify three selected subdivisions in relation to the river corridor.
- Produce a cartographic map illustrating the spatial relationships between development and riparian areas.

## Study Area

The study focuses on a section of the East Gallatin River near Bozeman, Montana.

Three subdivisions are highlighted:

1. Riverside Greens
2. Riverside Manor
3. Walker Property Subdivision

The map also displays parcels within two miles of the study area.

## Data Sources

- Gallatin County GIS
- Montana State Library
- City of Bozeman GIS
- 2024 Bozeman aerial imagery

## Methods

The project was completed using ArcGIS Pro.

1. Integrated spatial datasets representing waterways, parcels, structures, and selected subdivisions.
2. Displayed the 300-foot setback along the East Gallatin River.
3. Mapped parcels and structures surrounding the river corridor.
4. Highlighted three selected subdivisions for spatial comparison.
5. Used 2024 aerial imagery to provide geographic context.
6. Created a final cartographic layout with a legend, north arrow, scale bar, and data-source information.

## Map Interpretation

The map illustrates the spatial relationships between the East Gallatin River, its 300-foot setback, surrounding parcels, and existing structures.

The selected subdivisions are located near the river corridor, demonstrating the importance of considering riparian setbacks in land-use planning.

The map provides a visual basis for examining development patterns near riparian areas. It does not independently establish regulatory compliance or quantify the number of affected parcels.
